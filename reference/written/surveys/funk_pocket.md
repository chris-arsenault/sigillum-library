# Funk pocket without a drum kit (survey, source-cited)

The problem: a groove piece for acoustic instruments and no percussion that
must read as funk. The first Boreal Pocket draft (July 2026) failed it in a
way that generalizes: every low part attacked beat 1, there was no backbeat,
the "ghost notes" doubled the bass an octave below, and the featured bass
voice walked a diatonic scale. This survey records what a funk pocket is
made of and how to assign it to pitched instruments so it stays music rather
than a bad drum imitation. Sources are in the Boreal Pocket research record
(`sigillum-explorations/explorations/boreal_pocket/docs/04_funk_bass_research.md`)
and are cited inline here by name; verify against the record before quoting.

## 1. The unit is the 16th, and the One is weighted by what surrounds it

Funk's basic pulse unit is the 16th (Hein on "Cold Sweat"). The sparsest
workable cell is three attacks: beat 1, the "a" of 2, beat 3 (eBassGuitar).
Beat 1 is heavy because the material around it is light, and it is often
weighted by anticipation: the last 16th of the previous bar sounds the
downbeat's pitch and ties across ("What Is Hip?", Castillo in Mix). Shared
downbeats can reinforce this weight. Judge their relationship to the lighter
surrounding attacks; the number of players on beat 1 is not a groove test.

Which 16ths get real notes: anticipations of 2 and 3 by a 16th, the "a"
before the next downbeat, and kicks scattered on the "e"s and "a"s (Hein on
"There Was A Time"; Loudlands on "Cissy Strut"). The bass leaves 2 and 4 to
the snare rather than doubling them; a long note stops just before the snare
(TalkBass, unverified quote).

Two dialects that matter for composing: Prestia's even, half-muted 16ths
with the "Rocco gallop" (8th + two 16ths, or two 16ths + 8th, per beat) and
Zender's fingerstyle verse locked to the kick with slap for the chorus. Both
players say the identity is muting and ghost notes, not note count.

## 2. Pitch grammar

Dominant-7 vocabulary (root, 3, 5, b7) plus chromatic approaches: 6 into 5,
2 into 3, major 7 passing between b7 and root (TalkingBass). The root–b7–
octave box (eBassGuitar). "The major 6th to minor 7th move is very popular"
(TalkingBass on "Good Times"). Prestia: octave jumps, chromatic approach
into roots, root–fifth–octave shapes, a drop to the major third below the
root, one foreign pitch used as a section signal. Riffs are one- or two-bar
cells; the second bar answers the first with chromatic motion and returns
via 6/4/5 ("Cosmic Girl" verse cell). One-chord vamps are normal ("Sex
Machine"). In D dorian the 6→b7 move is B→C, the chromatic approach into the
fifth is Bb→A, and the third below the root is Bb1: the dorian/aeolian
flat-six argument can therefore be planted as bass ornaments before it is
ever stated harmonically.

## 3. Phrase-level variation

Four-bar groove with a fill in bar 4, or 8-bar riff with a 2-bar fill.
Variation set: the same sequence up or down an octave, phrase end on the
5th as turnaround, chord tones doubled as 16ths, long quarters against 16th
figures for light and shade, sweeteners added sparingly over an unchanged
foundation (TalkingBass, eBassGuitar). Edwards goes busier in choruses by
accenting the higher octave. Zender lays out for a whole verse before
entering. Breakdowns strip to the drummer alone (Brown) or the bass alone
(Jamiroquai live).

## 4. Space

Prestia: "If you're overplaying, you're under-listening." Edwards: a part
simple enough to sing, leaving space for the ensemble. The counter-example
is "Virtual Insanity": a walking cycle-of-fifths line over a three-bar
pattern with a 2/4 turnaround, which is a different dialect (Chic, Don
Blackman), not a vamp.

## 5. Assigning the kit to pitched players

Larry Graham invented slap with no drummer: thumb for the kick, pluck for
the snare. The lesson is that the kit is a set of attack placements, not a
set of instruments. Four functions, four subdivisions, four players:

| Function | Attack placement | How it stays melodic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bass line and hook | The One (often anticipated), "a" of 2, beat 3, ghosts on "e"s | It is the riff |
| Kick | Only the riff's anchor pitches, an octave below | The riff's skeleton; never a continuous line |
| Backbeat | 2 and 4, one anticipation per period | A two- or three-note answer whose pitches carry the 9th and 13th: the horn answer in one instrument |
| Chank | The "e"s and "&"s in one player, the "a"s and "&"s in another | Rhythm-guitar voicings carrying 9, 11, 13; each part mostly rests; together they tile |

These are possible assignments, not exclusive lanes. Bass and kick can
coincide; bass can answer the snare or reinforce a ghost; several players
can voice a chord on the same attack. Keep a perceptible clock and accent
hierarchy before distributing syncopation. Do not require an ensemble hole
every four bars or prohibit three parts sharing a rhythm. A repeated groove
can remain the foundation while melody, harmony and phrase weight develop.

On strings: the chop (a vertical drop-and-lift stroke) carries a backbeat
with hard/soft combinations (Strings Magazine; Turtle Island; Driessen's chop
notation); cello pizz grooves put the bass line in the thumb and open-string
ghosts in the fingers (Tsabbah); orchestral pizz above E5 loses resonance
and three-part pizz 16ths above about six plucks per second stop being
clean. On bassoon: Paul Hanson treats the instrument as a guitar a fourth
lower that can "bop between bass notes and higher notes"; ghosts are
low-air short staccato; there is no usable slap tongue with the reed on;
volume is the limit, so the accompaniment under a bassoon riff must be pizz
or light.

## 6. Horn-section role for strings

Tower of Power's horns were written "almost as part of the rhythm section":
staccato unison hits, the horns taking the up notes the bass takes on the
high strings, 7#9 vocabulary (Adams, in Mix). For strings playing that role:
unison punches can answer the bass's rests or reinforce selected attacks.
Voice the chord at the actual attack: a ninth somewhere in a labelled bar
does not establish a sounded ninth chord. Extend or release the voicing to
support the foreground phrase, and vary its place in the paragraph rather
than rationing punches to a prescribed half-section.

## 7. Section contrast inside one groove piece

Three dialects that differ in subdivision, not only in who plays: straight
syncopated 16ths (Brown, Tower of Power), straight four-on-the-floor 8ths
with root–octave pumps and chank on the "&"s (Chic, Jamiroquai "Alright"),
and swung 16ths (Meters, New Orleans; at 90–104 bpm). Subdivision change is
one source of contrast. A stable grid can also support a distinct melody,
harmonic destination, drum break or transfer of initiative. Swung 16ths in Partitura
are `1/3` + `1/6` quarter fractions and export as a triplet-eighth plus
triplet-16th under one bracket per beat.

## Coverage

Written September 17, 2026; revised after the user rejected the first
Boreal Pocket rebuild. Its no-shared-attacks recipes had encouraged
disconnected fragments and chord labels without voicings. They are not
accepted proof points.

The user's subsequent eight-bar bass and one-bar kit MIDI supply a concrete
counterexample: a repeating eighth-note hat clock, loud backbeats, quiet
snare ghosts, and a bass head that meets the ghost placements rather than
merely doubling kick. Its repeated head, Eb answers, octave flares and Ab
exit form a phrase. Their electric bass and kit are writing vehicles, not an
ensemble instruction. The commission puts the bass hook in bassoon and permits
sparse concert percussion. Strings still need melodic and harmonic work rather
than becoming a collection of drum substitutes.

For a clipped percussive chant, first establish a recognizable repeated-note
rhythm and consonant/rest pattern. Shortening a sung chord arpeggio may
leave its melodic identity unchanged. Wider pitches can punctuate that
rhythm without forcing a new vocal contour on every chord change.

No new executable card is claimed. The recomposition is a candidate
realization awaiting listening judgment. Use exact verticals and phrase
reading alongside `beat_salience` and `ensemble_grid`; detector counts do
not certify funk. Unpitched drum-map keys must not enter tonal analysis as
sounding notes, and spectral estimates must disclose missing drum models.

The later orchestral revision was also rejected: too many surprise rhythms,
no dependable anchor, dispersed harmony, and a middle dominated by held notes.
Do not answer that diagnosis by manufacturing more local variation. Establish
a repeatable phrase and its pulse; let the accompaniment repeat while the
melody and harmonic destination develop. A shared downbeat, a familiar
backbeat and an actual sustained voicing can be the right material. Inspect
accent recurrence as well as note recurrence. Broad contrast belongs to an
intentional passage, not to every passage that relinquishes its accompaniment.
